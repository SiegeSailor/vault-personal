- $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime if and only if $gcd(m, n) = 1$
- Smallest positiver integer $x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n$ is a divisor of $m$ and $n$
  - $m \mod (x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n) = 0$
  - $n \mod (x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n) = 0$
  - Every divisor $d$ of $m$ and $n$ is a divisor of $x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n$
    - $m \mod d = 0$
    - $n \mod d = 0$
    - $(x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n) \mod d = 0$
    - $x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n$ is the greatest common divisor of $m$ and $n$
    - If $x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n = 1$, then $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime numbers
- $\phi(n)$ is the number of integers from $1$ to $n$ being relatively prime to $n$
  - $n = p_1^{e_1} \times p_2^{e_2} \times ... \times p_n^{e_n}$, then $\phi(n) = (p_1 - 1)p_1^{e_1 - 1} \times (p_2 - 1)p_1^{e_2 - 1} \times ... \times (p_n - 1)p_1^{e_n - 1}$
    - $= n \times (1 - \frac{1}{p_1}) \times (1 - \frac{1}{p_2}) \times ... \times (1 - \frac{1}{p_n})$
  - $n = 1$, then $\phi(n) = 1$
  - $n$ is prime, then $\phi(n) = n - 1$
  - $n$ is prime with $k$ exponents, $n = p^k$, then $\phi(n) = p^k - p^{k - 1}$
  - $n$ is a product of primes, $n = p_1 \times p_2$, then $\phi(n) = \phi(p_1) \times \phi(p_2)$
    - Related to Chinese Remainder
- $p$ is prime. If $p$ divides $a \times b$, then $p$ divides $a$ or $p$ divides $b$
  - If $p$ divides $a_1 \times a_2 \times ... \times a_k$, then $p$ divides at least one of $a_k$'s
- Any integer can be presented as the product of prime numbers
  - Every integer's representation as the product of prime numbers is unique
- Eratasthenes's Sieve: An algorithm to find all prime numbers less than $n$
- For 2 positive integers $m$ and $n$, $gcd(m, n) = gcd(n, m \mod n)$
- **Euclidean**: Find greatest common divisor of $m$ and $n$. If result is $1$, then $m$ and $n$ are relative prime
- **Expanded Euclidean**: Find greatest common divisor of $m$ and $n$ in $x_0 \times m + y_0 \times n$ form
- **Multiplicative Inverses**: Find multiplicative $y_1, y_2, ..., y_h$ inverses of $x \mod m$ if $(x \times y) \mod m = 1$
  - If $x$ has multiplicative inverses $\mod m$, then $x$ is relatively prim to $m$

  - If $y$ and $y'$ are multiplicative inverses of $x \mod m$, then $y - y' = k \times m$

- Relation $R$ is a subset of $S \times S$, where $S \times S$ is a product set. 2 element $x$ and $y$ are called related if and only if $(x, y) \in R$
  - A relation is called an equivalence relation if and only if:
    - Reflexive: $x \sim x$
    - Symmetric: If $x \sim y$, then $y \sim x$ are related
    - Transitive: If $x \sim y$ and $x \sim z$, then $x \sim z$
  - An equivalence block of a relation $R$ generated by $x$ is the set $\overline{x} = \{ y \in S | y \sim x, \ \textnormal{for every} \ y \in S \}$
    - For every $x \in \overline{x}$, form a partition of $S$
  - If $a \in \overline{x} \cap \overline{y}$, then $\overline{x} = \overline{y}$
  - Define a relation as $x \sim y \iff x - y = m \times k$. If $x \sim y$, then $x \equiv y \mod m$
    - $\sim$ is an equivalence relation
  - $\overline{x} = x \mod m = \{ y \in Z | y = x \mod m \}$
  - Set $Z$ is the set of blocks $\{ \overline{0}, \overline{1}, \overline{2}, ..., \overline{m - 1} \}$
  - $x' \equiv x \mod m$ and $y' \equiv y \mod m$, that is, $x'$ and $x$ belong to the same block, $y'$ and $y$ as well:
    - $x' + y' \equiv (x + y) \mod m$. $x' + y'$ is in the same block with $x + y$
    - $x' \times y' \equiv x \times y \mod m$
    - $\overline{x} + \overline{y} = \overline{x + y}$ and $\overline{x} \times \overline{y} = \overline{x \times y}$
    - $(x + y) \mod m= (x \mod m + y \mod m) \mod m$

- If $x$ and $y$ are relative prime to $m$, then $x \times y$ is relative prime to $m$
- If $x$ and $y$ are multiplicative inverses to $m$, then $x \times y$ is multiplicative inverse to $m$

## Lecture 2

- The set $Z^+_m$ of integers modulo $m$ relatively prime to $m$, with the operation of addition $\mod m$ is an abelian group
  - $Z_m = \{ \overline{0}, \overline{1}, \overline{2}, ..., \overline{m - 1} \}$
  - $\overline{x} + \overline{y} = \overline{x + y}$, for any $\overline{x}, \overline{y} \in Z_m$
  - Identity element is $\overline{0}$
  - The inverse for any $\overline{x}$ is $\overline{(m - x)}$
  - Associative and commutative
- The set $Z^*_m$ of integers modulo $m$ relatively prime to $m$, with the operation of multiplication $\mod m$ is an abelian group
  - $Z_m = \{ \overline{0}, \overline{1}, \overline{2}, ..., \overline{m - 1} \}$
  - $\overline{x} \times \overline{y} = \overline{x \times y}$, for any $\overline{x}, \overline{y} \in Z_m$
  - Identity element is $\overline{1}$
  - The inverse for an $\overline{x}$ is an element $\overline{y}$, such that $\overline{x} \times \overline{y} = 1$
  - Associative and abelian
- $H$ is a subgroup of $G$. For any $g \in H : g \times g^{-1} = e$ as $e$ is an element of $G$ and $H$ is closed under the group $G$ operation, so $e \in H$
- Lagrange: $G$ is a finite group. The order of any subgroup divides the order of the group
  - $|G| = 8$, then the order of any subgroup $\in \{ 1, 2, 4, 8 \}$
  - The number of cosets of $H$ in $G$ equals $\frac{|G|}{|H|}$
- $G$ is a finite group, The set of power of $g$ is $\langle g \rangle = \{ e, g^1, g^2, ..., g^{n - 1} \}$
  - $\langle g \rangle$ is a subset of $G$
  - $\langle g \rangle$ is a cyclic group generated by $g$
  - $|g| = |\langle g \rangle|$, and it divides $|G|$
  - For all numbers generated by $g$, there is the least common multiple, $l$
  - The exponent of a group $G$ is the least common multiple of all the $|\langle g \rangle|$
  - For every element $g \in G$, $g^l = e$
  - |G| is divisible by $l$

### Diffie-Hellman

1. `Alice` and `Bob` agree public generator $g = 3$ and prime modulus $p = 17$
2. `Alice` select a private random number $15$
   - Run FastExponentiation $3^{15} \mod 17 \cong 6$
   - Send $6$ to `Bob` and `Eve`
3. `Bob` select a private random number $13$
   - Run FastExponentiation $3^{13} \mod 17 \cong 12$
   - Send $12$ to `Alice` and `Eve`
4. `Alice` run FastExponentiation $12^{15} \mod 17 \cong 10$
   - $15$ is previous chosen random number
   - $12$ is from `Bob`
   - This is a shared secret
5. `Bob` run FastExponentiation $6^{13} \mod 17 \cong 10$
   - $13$ is previous chosen random number
   - $6$ is from `Alice`
   - This is a shared secret
6. Why the key is sufficient for them to understand each other:
   $$
   ((12 = 3^{13} \mod 17 \cong 12)^{15} \mod 17) \\
   (3^{13})^{15} \mod 17 \cong 10
   $$
   $$
   ((6 = 3^{15} \mod 17 \cong 6)^{13} \mod 17) \\
   (3^{15})^{13} \mod 17 \cong 10
   $$
7. Without one of the private random number $15$ and $13$, `Eve` is unable to find the solution due to Discrete Logarithm Problem

### Chinese Remainder

- $x \cong 2 (\mod 3)$
- $x \cong 2 (\mod 4)$
- $x \cong 1 (\mod 5)$
- Find $x$

$gcd(3, 4) = 1, gcd(4, 5) = 1, gcd(5, 3) = 1$. We are good to use Chinese Remainder:

$$
x = \neg \mod 3 + \neg \mod 4 + \neg \mod 5 \\
x = 4 \times 5 + 3 \times 5 + 3 \times 4 = 20 + 15 + 12
$$

$x = 146$. Any product of $3 \times 4 \times 5$ plus $x$ is also the $x$. For example, $x = 146 + 60, x = 146 + 120, ...$
